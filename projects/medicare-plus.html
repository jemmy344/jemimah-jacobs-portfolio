01 — The business challenge

A private hospital takes card payments at reception and through an online patient portal, passing card data to a third-party processor. Separately, staff were viewing patient medical records they had no need to see.

02 — My work

For the payment incident, I mapped the applicable PCI DSS v4.0.1 requirements to the hospital's set-up and recommended how to protect cardholder data. For the records incident, I confirmed the data was protected health information (PHI), identified the applicable HIPAA safeguards and wrote a remediation plan.

03 — Key recommendations

PCI
Shrink the scope first

Use PCI-validated point-to-point encryption (P2PE) terminals at reception and hosted payment fields or a redirect on the portal, so card data never touches the hospital's network or servers.

PCI
Never store the CVV; segment the network

No security codes after authorisation; isolate the payment environment from clinical, IT and guest networks; verify each provider's Attestation of Compliance.

HIPAA
Role-based access and minimum necessary

Billing sees billing fields only; clinicians see only patients in their care unit; elevated access needs manager approval.

HIPAA
Audit logging, alerts and re-training

Log every view, edit or export of PHI, alert on out-of-shift or high-profile access, and re-train staff with real consequences for snooping.

04 — Key insight

Logging alone doesn't stop snooping, and training alone doesn't either. The plan combines prevention (role-based access), detection (audit alerts) and deterrence (sanctions and training), so each covers the others' gaps.

05 — Outcome and limits

An individual training assessment presenting requirement mapping and recommendations. No implementation or audit outcome is claimed.
