01 — The business challenge

An external data protection audit found a fintech start-up (a cloud invoicing and bookkeeping app with open-banking bank sync) "substantially compliant: improvements and documentary evidence required" across ten areas. Most controls had been reported but could not yet be demonstrated, and there were specific gaps such as no legitimate interest assessments.

02 — My work

I built a 30-document compliance programme that maps every audit evidence requirement to a tailored policy, register or assessment. Each document was written for the company's actual product, with unconfirmed facts flagged rather than assumed, and a separate checklist of the real evidence still needed (signed approvals, MFA and encryption screenshots, penetration test reports, signed processor agreements).

03 — Featured deliverables

DPIA
Automatic bank sync and payment matching

Six risks assessed, from account takeover to data kept after a user disconnects. Key design control: automated matches are never finalised without user confirmation.

LIA
Security logging and monitoring

A three-part purpose, necessity and balancing test that closes a specific audit finding, with safeguards against scope creep into employee monitoring.

ROPA
Record of Processing Activities

Nine activities with lawful basis, recipients, retention and owners, identifying where the company acts as a processor rather than a controller.

PACK
Breach, rights, retention, transfers and processors

Breach procedure and register, DSAR procedure, retention schedule, international transfer risk assessment, processor due diligence and DPA template.

04 — How it maps to UK GDPR

  • ROPA → Article 30 · Lawful basis → Article 6 · LIA → Article 6(1)(f)
  • DPIA → Article 35 · Privacy by design → Article 25
  • Breach response → Articles 33–34 (ICO within 72 hours)
  • Processors → Article 28 · International transfers → Chapter V (IDTA and TRA)

05 — Outcome and limits

The documents are v0.9 drafts prepared for management and DPO approval. They are clearly marked as drafts until approved and implemented, separating written policy from demonstrated, evidenced control.
